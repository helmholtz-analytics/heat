# Project Governance

As a project hosted under Linux Foundation Europe, Heat's technical oversight is managed by a Technical Steering Committee (TSC). The TSC has the authority to define and amend community roles as long as they are publicly documented here in the CONTRIBUTING file.

Participation in Heat is open to any individual or organization that abides by the Technical Charter and project policies, regardless of competitive interests. The project operates in an open, transparent, and non-discriminatory manner.

### Contributors

**Contributors** include anyone in the technical community who contributes code, documentation, issue reports, reviews, or other technical artifacts to the project.

### Maintainers

For the Heat project, we use the title **Maintainer** to represent the "Committer" role defined in the project's Technical Charter.

- **Definition:** Maintainers are Contributors who have earned the ability to modify ("commit") source code, documentation, and other technical artifacts directly within the project's repository, and who handle day-to-day pull request reviews and merges.
- **Becoming a Maintainer:** Participation is open to anyone who abides by the project charter. A Contributor may be promoted to a Maintainer through a majority approval vote by the existing Maintainers, with confirmation by the TSC.
- **Removal:** If necessary, a Maintainer may be removed from their role by a majority approval vote of the other existing Maintainers or the TSC.

### Technical Steering Committee (TSC)

The **Technical Steering Committee (TSC)** oversees Heat's overall technical direction, releases, and governance under the Technical Charter. TSC voting members and Maintainers are distinct roles and do not necessarily overlap.

- **Membership:** TSC voting members may be added or removed by a majority vote of all current TSC voting members.
- **Meetings:** The TSC meets periodically (typically quarterly or as needed) separately from the team's regular development and planning calls. TSC meetings are open to the public and may be conducted electronically, via teleconference, or in person. Routine coordination and electronic votes also take place openly through GitHub Issues and GitHub Discussions.
- **TSC chair:** The TSC may elect a Chair to preside over TSC meetings and serve until resignation or replacement by the TSC. The Chair (or another TSC member designated by the TSC) acts as the primary communication contact between Heat and the High Performance Software Foundation (HPSF).

### Decision making and voting

- **Consensus first:** Heat operates primarily as a consensus-based community, with Maintainers overseeing day-to-day code and pull request decisions.
- **Formal voting:** When consensus cannot be reached and a formal TSC decision is required, each TSC voting member has one vote.
- **Quorum:** At least 50% of all TSC voting members must be present to establish quorum at a meeting.
- **Approval thresholds:**
  - Decisions at a meeting with quorum require a majority vote of the voting members in attendance.
  - Electronic votes conducted outside a meeting require a majority vote of all TSC voting members.
  - Exceptions to the project's inbound/outbound license policy (Charter Section 7.c) and amendments to the Technical Charter require a two-thirds vote of the entire TSC (plus LF Europe approval for Charter amendments).
- **Escalation:** If a vote cannot be resolved by the TSC, any voting member may refer the matter to LF Europe for assistance in reaching a resolution.

### Code of conduct and foundation policies

All Contributors, Maintainers, and community participants must adhere to the [Heat Code of Conduct](https://heat.readthedocs.io/en/stable/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.html) as well as applicable [Linux Foundation Europe policies](https://linuxfoundation.eu/policies/).

## Legal and intellectual property requirements

To comply with the Heat Technical Charter under Linux Foundation Europe, all contributions to the Project are subject to the following policies:

- **Copyright retention:** The copyright in all new contributions is retained by the copyright holder as independent works of authorship; no copyright assignment is required.
- **Code licensing:** All new inbound code contributions must be made under the MIT License, and contributed source files should include an `SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT` header.
- **Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO):** All new inbound code contributions must include a [Developer Certificate of Origin](http://developercertificate.org) sign-off (`Signed-off-by`), binding the contributor and, if applicable, their employer to the license.
- **Documentation licensing:** Documentation is received and made available under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC-BY-4.0) License.
- **Trademarks:** LF Europe holds title to all Project Trademarks, and any use by Collaborators must follow LF Europe's trademark guidelines.
